Can imitation handle a rougher start?

For releases and kicks from the top, open the top-recovery experiment →

Starting angles and velocities are independently randomized around hanging down. Every neural policy acts from the current state, with no clock or deterministic fallback. This tests starting disturbances, not kicks delivered after reaching the top.

We compare three existing radial neural policies with twelve variants fitted using additional successful teacher recovery demonstrations. The teacher baseline advances its trajectory clock throughout recovery. Extra demonstrations use separate training starts; only rollouts that finish balanced are kept.

The original imitation policy already works well here.

Trajectory 1 passed 767 of 768 fresh starting disturbances with finer physics, including 128/128 in every larger-disturbance category. Its one failure was in the smallest category. Adding recovery demonstrations did not improve aggregate success in this comparison.

For ±1 rad / ±2 rad/s starts, median sustained balance began around 27 seconds; for velocity-only ±4 rad/s starts, around 30 seconds. These are sampled results, not a guarantee for every state.
