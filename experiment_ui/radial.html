A neural policy, from ground to balance.

New: larger starting disturbances and recovery imitation →

This experiment trains a radial-basis neural network on successful controller demonstrations. Its Gaussian hidden units combine locally fitted torque predictions. At runtime, a standalone neural graph takes the current angles and velocities and outputs torque. There is no trajectory clock or LQR fallback.

This is specialized neural imitation, not PPO or the earlier shared MLP. Prototype locations come from training examples; local torque weights are learned by least squares. The actuator remains bounded to ±1 N m, with a 50 ms control interval.

Independent two-minute tests

Success requires the final ten seconds continuously balanced: both links within 10° of upright and both absolute link speeds below 0.5 rad/s. Fresh tests use a separate random seed. The ±0.01, ±0.05 and ±0.1 tests perturb every initial angle and velocity; episode 0 is exactly motionless at the bottom. These tests do not establish global disturbance recovery.
